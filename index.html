Thesis — Machine Learning-Based Mechanical Properties Modeling of Steel Fiber-Reinforced Concrete

Adding hooked industrial steel fibers to concrete improves its tensile and flexural strength, but the fibers' effect on compressive strength was still an open question. This thesis used machine learning and deep learning to predict the compressive strength of steel fiber-reinforced concrete from 176 data points collected across the open literature. A sensitivity analysis showed that water-to-cement ratio and fine-aggregate content most reduce compressive strength, while superplasticizer, fly ash, and cement content increase it; aggregate size and fiber length-to-diameter ratio contributed least. Model performance was compared using R², RMSE, and MAE — a convolutional neural network performed best (R² = 0.928, RMSE = 5.043, MAE = 3.833), while k-nearest neighbour performed weakest (R² = 0.881, RMSE = 6.477, MAE = 4.648).
